1 A hull in three views

A lines plan describes a curved hull on flat paper. Imagine slicing the hull with three sets of parallel planes. Each set is drawn in the view where it shows its true shape:

  • Sheer plan: from the side
  • Half-breadth plan: from above (one half; the hull is symmetrical)
  • Body plan: end-on, fore body on one side and after body on the other

2 Stations

Vertical slices across the hull at regular intervals. Their true shape is the curve in the body plan; in the sheer and half-breadth plans they are straight lines. Frames were often moulded from them.

3 Waterlines

Horizontal slices at set heights above the baseline. Their true shape is the curve in the half-breadth plan. The load waterline (LWL) is where the hull floats when loaded; Steffy’s method puts it at about 3/5 of the depth amidships.

4 Buttock lines

Vertical slices running fore-and-aft, parallel to the centreline. Their true shape is the curve in the sheer plan; they show how the bottom sweeps up towards bow and stern.

5 Diagonals

Inclined fore-and-aft planes cutting through the turn of the bilge. Straight in the body plan, their developed shape is plotted in the half-breadth plan, opposite the waterlines. Draughtsmen used them to check that the bilge was fair.

6 Steffy’s conventions

Maritime archaeologists following J. R. Steffy draw lines plans in a consistent way, so reconstructions can be compared:

  • The bow is to the left
  • The plans are titled Sheer, Half-Breadth and Body
  • Sections are lettered A, B, C … forward of the midship section and numbered 1, 2, 3 … aft of it
  • The midship section carries the amidships mark ⦶

7 Fair lines

A hull is fair when its curves change smoothly, with no bumps, kinks or flat spots. Shipwrights bent long wooden battens, held by lead weights called ducks, through the measured points to find a fair curve.

Shiplines does the same with a subdivision surface, the technique behind Blender’s Subdivision Surface modifier. You move a cage of control handles and the smooth hull follows. Zebra stripes show at a glance where it is lumpy.
